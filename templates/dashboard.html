THE CHALLENGE IN MINERAL DATA

Why Mineral Exploration Data is Difficult to Work With

Mineral exploration across India has generated decades of valuable technical documentation, yet researchers, geologists, and students encounter severe workflow hurdles when trying to extract actionable insights.

Scattered & Siloed Records

Crucial geological exploration data remains buried across disparate PDF reports, physical bulletins, and isolated institutional archives, requiring hours of manual collation.

Multi-Analyte Ambiguity

In raw tables, one physical rock or core sample frequently spawns multiple rows (Al2O3, SiO2, Fe2O3, TiO2). Naive statistical parsing incorrectly conflates analyte counts with physical sample counts.

Inconsistent Coordinates

Exploration blocks often document a single regional or lease reference coordinate rather than surveyed collar positions for every borehole, leading to inaccurate spatial representations.

Complex Grade Interpretations

Comparing mineralized intervals across distinct geological formations requires careful unit harmonization (percent vs. ppm) and transparent separation of commercial ore thresholds.

Laborious Reporting Workflows

Drafting exploration summaries, site comparisons, and audit dossiers manually requires repetitive data extraction and chart formatting without unified audit trails.

Lack of Standardized Tooling

Mining teams and academic departments often oscillate between unwieldy spreadsheets and high-cost proprietary GIS software lacking customized mineral exploration logic.
